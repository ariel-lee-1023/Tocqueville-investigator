# The old regime: continuity, division, and revolutionary preparation

Use this module when an apparent break conceals institutional continuity, when reform provokes resistance, or when people become more alike while their common life diminishes. The register is public historical analysis. The main question is how practices and relations developed before the moment that later actors identified as a new beginning.

## Start below the declared rupture

A regime can abolish names and privileges while retaining expectations about how power should work. Identify who handled a class of business before and after the change, to whom inhabitants applied, and which remedies were actually open. The Revolution did not invent every centralizing practice later carried in its name. The question is not whether the new arrangement resembles the old in an abstract way, but which functions, habits, and dependencies crossed the break.

This inquiry begins in the preface's move from famous books to neglected works, instructions to representatives, and administrative correspondence. Polished public arguments tell us what their authors wanted others to believe. A request to an administrator can expose what a person hoped, feared, or required permission to do. Neither kind of material is discarded; they illuminate different aspects of society. The archive is not merely a repository of legal form. It can show the relation between the governed and the governing in operation.

An application should name the surviving practice. “The past persists” is too easy a conclusion. A municipal body that continues to exist while losing discretion illustrates one kind of survival; an administrative procedure taken over by a new regime illustrates another. Distinguish institutional name, personnel, effective competence, and the public's expectation of assistance. A change in one does not establish a change in all.

## A power can expand beneath the old authorities

The crown did not have to demolish every local body in order to displace its work. An intendant, dependent on the center, could acquire effective control while municipalities and other authorities retained their visible forms. The result was overlapping institutions and a concentration of decision rather than a simple diagram of one sovereign issuing commands to an empty landscape.

Trace the practical consequences. Local actors learn to seek permission and intervention; the center receives requests for matters it cannot understand as those on the spot do; delay and exception become ordinary features of administration. Formal power can be extensive and its exercise irregular. An institution can be intrusive in small matters and timid when it meets resistance. A description of centralization must therefore account for habits of action, bargaining, and hesitation as well as legal scope.

This mechanism is useful when a reform changes the diagram but leaves incentives to petition intact. Ask whether the local actor now controls money and execution, can refuse instructions, and can be held responsible by those affected. Delegating labor while retaining all discretion does not establish local independence. Equally, retaining national authority over a genuinely common interest does not itself establish administrative tutelage.

## Similarity can coexist with estrangement

The nobility and the increasingly prosperous commoner could become closer in education and tastes while remaining divided by privileges, taxes, and corporate boundaries. The separation was not simply a measure of how different their lives looked. Rank could lose a public function while preserving a private advantage; its beneficiaries then appeared less like governors who rendered a service and more like isolated recipients of dues.

The comparison with England tests the relation between privilege and common political action. Shared participation can keep classes in contact even when they remain unequal. Exemption and administrative separation can estrange people even as their conditions converge. The explanatory variable is the work they do with and for one another, not the visual distance between their lifestyles.

Feudal rights became especially odious where the peasant owned land and the lord no longer performed the governing functions that had accompanied his claims. The burden was read in a changed relation. This does not turn every obsolete privilege into a valid arrangement in earlier times; it explains why the same exaction can change its political meaning. For a present institution, ask what justifying service has disappeared while the charge or precedence remains.

## Why improvement can precede revolt

A reduction in suffering can make remaining suffering more visible. Once people imagine a practicable alternative, learn that the authorities can change an arrangement, and gain more room to compare or communicate, burdens previously accepted can become intolerable. The old regime's late reforms therefore cannot be understood by assuming revolt rises in direct proportion to misery.

This is a conditional mechanism, not a law that benevolent reform always causes revolution. Identify which expectations changed, which obstacles remain, and why the possibility of removing them now seems near. Reform can also acquire legitimacy and create durable means of participation. The judgment depends on its execution and on whether authority retains credibility while altering the expectations on which compliance rested.

The argument also directs attention to sequencing. If the old agents lose their authority before the new bodies acquire experience, resources, or a workable division of functions, confusion can be created by the reform itself. The pre-revolutionary administrative reorganization is a case in which legal innovation disturbed practical relations. A proposal for decentralization must ask what responsibility is learned, when it is transferred, and how disputes are settled during the transition.

## Men of letters and the experience of governing

When educated people cannot participate in public affairs, political intelligence can move toward complete and symmetrical schemes. The attraction of a general plan is strengthened by exclusion from the details that would reveal its limits. Institutions and intellectual habits interact: removing practical responsibility can help produce the very political abstraction later cited as a reason to distrust the public.

Do not convert this into hostility to thought or books. The investigator himself works through ideas and books. The distinction concerns how an idea is tested and what kind of attention its author has learned. The appropriate corrective is some contact with responsibility, friction, and particular interests. A plan that treats every intermediate obstacle as an irrational remnant may abolish the forces capable of limiting its own executor.

## What remains of the judgment on 1789

The early aspiration to secure rights and freedom has a dignity that is not cancelled by later events. Nor does that dignity require admiration of every revolutionary practice. A historical account can trace inherited centralization, identify costly destruction of existing liberties, and still refuse to restore the privileges of the old society.

The question for a new case is which older arrangements contain independent powers worth preserving, which perpetuate unjust exclusion, and what could replace their public functions. An answer that says simply to preserve tradition or to sweep it away has stopped before the difficult discrimination. The old regime's lesson is a method for finding what is being inherited and what is being lost, not a universal verdict against reform.
